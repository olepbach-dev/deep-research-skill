# Competitor research

## Define the unit of comparison

Clarify whether the competitor is being evaluated as:

- A product and feature set
- A substitute for a job-to-be-done
- A positioning/messaging strategy
- A pricing and packaging model
- A user experience
- A go-to-market motion

Do not compare competitors only by feature count.

Use first-party pages for what the competitor claims and independent/user sources for whether reality matches.

## Three analytical passes

1. **Map:** what exists, for whom, and how it is sold.
2. **Challenge:** gaps, contradictions, exclusions, operational limitations, and weak proof.
3. **Implications:** where to match, differentiate, avoid competing, or gather more evidence.

This is a reasoning sequence, not a mandatory report style.

## Coverage and recommendation audit

Before finalizing:

- Reconcile the landscape list, source manifest, profiles, and comparison matrix. Every decision-relevant competitor must be profiled or explicitly labeled adjacent, substitute, excluded, or insufficiently evidenced.
- Recheck mutable facts such as price, beta status, store-user counts, and examples. Timestamp them; use a range or qualitative description when repeated retrievals disagree.
- Separate **technical feasibility**, **platform-policy compliance**, **credential/account security**, and **proven user outcomes**. A competitor workaround establishes only what it directly demonstrates.
- Separate recommendations supported by outcome evidence from hypotheses about time savings, reduced friction, or distribution. Label the latter as experiments with a validation gate.
- Do not convert a foreign B2B example into a validated local distribution strategy without local buyer evidence.

## Output

Include:

- Decision and scope
- Short competitor profiles
- Comparable evidence matrix
- Observed strengths and weaknesses
- Customer-language themes, labeled as signals
- Whitespace/opportunities with reasoning
- Risks and unknowns
- Recommended next actions
- Reconciliation ledger for material challenges: accepted, partial, or declined; original-source basis; report change
- Source manifest

Avoid adversarial language such as "what to fear." Focus on decision-relevant advantages, constraints, and evidence.
